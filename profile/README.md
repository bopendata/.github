# B-OPEN

**Birmingham Open-Source Datasets (B-OPEN)** is an open-source, local and multi-domain geospatial data platform developed to organize, document, and share research-ready spatial datasets for the Birmingham metropolitan area, Alabama.

B-OPEN addresses a common challenge in spatial research: while large amounts of public and open data are available from municipal, state, federal, and Earth observation sources, these datasets often require substantial processing, integration, and interpretation before they can be used in research. B-OPEN provides a shared platform for publishing the resulting derived datasets, spatial indicators, interactive maps, and supporting documentation so that they can be discovered, accessed, and reused by researchers across disciplines.

## What B-OPEN Provides

The platform brings together datasets from multiple domains within a common geographic context. Each project is developed using publicly available data and transparent, open-source geospatial workflows.

Repositories may include:

* **Research-ready spatial datasets** derived from publicly available sources
* **Spatial indicators** for environmental, transportation, infrastructure, and socioeconomic applications
* **Earth observation-derived products**
* **Interactive web maps and dashboards**
* **Metadata and documentation** describing data sources and processing methods
* **Downloadable data products** for further research and analysis

The goal is to make derived spatial information easier to discover, understand, reproduce, and reuse beyond the original project in which it was developed.

## A Local and Multi-Domain Data Ecosystem

B-OPEN is designed around the concept of a **local and multi-domain research data ecosystem**. Rather than replacing established global repositories or domain-specific data platforms, B-OPEN provides a local integration layer for derived spatial information.

The Birmingham metropolitan area serves as the initial testbed for this approach. The platform brings together information relevant to environmental quality, public health, transportation, urban planning, infrastructure, and other research domains within a shared geographic context.

This structure allows researchers from different disciplines to access spatial indicators and derived datasets without having to independently repeat the same data discovery, preprocessing, and integration workflows.

## Open and Reproducible Workflows

B-OPEN emphasizes transparency and reproducibility. Project repositories provide information about the underlying data sources, processing workflows, derived products, and limitations of the datasets.

The platform is developed using open-source tools and publicly available data wherever possible, allowing the workflows and resulting datasets to be examined, reused, and adapted for other research applications.

## Future Development

B-OPEN is intended to evolve as an expanding research data ecosystem. Future datasets and contributions can extend the platform into additional environmental, health, transportation, socioeconomic, and infrastructure domains while maintaining a consistent approach to documentation, metadata, processing, and data distribution.

Although Birmingham serves as the initial geographic focus, the broader objective is to demonstrate a replicable approach for developing local, multi-domain research data ecosystems in other cities and regions.

## About

B-OPEN was developed around the Birmingham metropolitan area, Alabama, as a prototype for improving the accessibility, reusability, and reproducibility of derived spatial research data.

For detailed information about a dataset, including its sources, methodology, processing workflow, and limitations, please visit the corresponding project repository.
